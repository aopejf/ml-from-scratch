What is an option worth?

An option is the right to buy a share at a price agreed today, on a date in the future. You do not have to use it. That choice has a value, and working it out is one of the oldest problems in finance.

These five reactions are called the Greeks. Traders watch them more closely than the price, because they say what happens next.

The Greeks, without the formulas

A textbook gets each Greek by calculus, one formula at a time. I did not write those. In project 2 I built an engine that records every step of a sum and then works backwards through it to find how much each input mattered. It was built to train neural networks. I wrote the option formula in it once, ran it backwards, and all the Greeks came out.

Three ways to the same price

The formula is one way. A second is to cut the time into small steps where the share goes up a little or down a little, and work back from the end. A third is to imagine a great many futures and average what the option pays. They should agree, and they do.

The tree earns its place on the last two rows. The formula only handles an option you must hold to the end. The tree can price one you may use early, by asking at every step whether using it now beats waiting.

Where the formula is wrong

The formula takes one volatility for the share. So I ran it backwards on real prices: for each option trading that day, what volatility would the formula need to give the price people paid? If the formula described the world, every option would give the same answer and the black line below would be flat.

Futures from a model that used to draw digits

The formula assumes every day's move comes from the same bell curve. Markets do not move like that. Bad days are far bigger than a bell curve allows, and they come in runs.

In project 4 I built a diffusion model that makes handwritten digits out of static. I gave the same recipe a different picture to learn: 64 days of market moves laid out as an 8 by 8 grid, for every three-month stretch of the S&P 500 since 1950. Then I asked it for new ones.

It is not a perfect copy of the market. Its futures are calmer than history and it makes fewer of the deepest falls. I left that in the table.

This is a worked method. It is not advice to buy or sell anything, and options can lose their whole value.
