Who will miss their next payment?

A bank in Taiwan published the records of 30,000 credit card customers: what they owed, what they paid, how late they were, and whether they missed the next payment. About one in five did.

I trained a model to see it coming. I did not use a machine-learning library to do the learning. It runs on an engine and an optimizer I wrote myself, earlier in this repository. Then I worked out what a lender should do with the score, and what should change when a recession arrives.

How good is the model?

I kept 6,000 customers back and never let the model learn from them. Then I asked it to score them. The usual measure is AUC: take one customer who defaulted and one who paid, and see how often the model rates the defaulter as riskier. A coin gets 0.5. A perfect model gets 1.

The second check matters as much as the first. My lending rule compares the model's percentage with a break-even percentage, so the percentages have to mean what they say. They do.

Knowing when a recession has started

Official recession dates are announced about a year late, which is no use to a lender. So I built a second model and did not tell it what a recession is. Each month it sees three figures for the US economy: the change in unemployment, in jobs and in factory output. Its only instruction is to sort every month since 1953 into three moods. One of the moods it found is plainly "shrinking".

Why did this customer get this score?

A lender who turns someone down has to say why. The engine that trained the model can also be pointed at one customer and asked how much each fact about them moved their risk away from the average customer's. The pieces add up exactly to the score.

This shows a method. It is not a credit model anyone should lend with. The customers are from one bank in Taiwan in 2005 and the recession figures are American, because no public data has both. "Default" here means missing the next month's payment.

The model sees only how a customer has handled credit. Sex, age, marital status and education are in the data and I left them out.
